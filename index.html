What is a Gobo What is a Gobo

A gobo (short for "goes before optics") is a stencil or template placed in front of a light source to shape the light into patterns, textures, or images — commonly used in stage lighting, photography, and architecture.

Why Use Gobos?

Below you can see two scenes: one on the left looks dull and unnatural. The other is alive, shaped by shadow (aka gobo). Light isn't just for seeing — it's for feeling. We break it up with gobos to create contrast, and let your render speak volumes.

Speeds up Rendering

Adding actual objects (trees, windows, etc.) just to generate shadows significantly increases render times. A 60-second-per-frame scene may rise to ~75s. Gobos Master removes the need for extra geometry — adding only 1–2 seconds of render time.

Note on layers

Adding more than 4 layers of gobos does increase render times a bit — but a new powerful add-on feature is in the works to make it very fast. Stay tuned.
